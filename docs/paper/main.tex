\begin{abstract}
Late-time acceleration is inferred by fitting homogeneous Friedmann--Lema\^{i}tre--Robertson--Walker (FLRW) models to coarse summaries of an inhomogeneous universe. We ask when such a coarse description can be a closed dynamical law, and what a fitted dark-energy term means when it cannot. Using the lens, completion and packaging vocabulary of Six Birds Theory, we prove, with machine-checked Lean proofs, that an exact coarse law exists precisely when micro states with the same observable evolve to states with the same observable. Commutation of ``coarse-grain then evolve'' with ``evolve then coarse-grain'' is sufficient for closure but not necessary: measured on full states, non-commutation can occur while an exact coarse law exists, whereas measured on the observables it is an exact obstruction. Two toy models make the mechanism concrete. For nonlinear evolution observed through a mean, the observable mismatch equals the time step times the nonlinearity times the variance. For Newtonian dust patches that all decelerate, the volume average obeys $3\ddot a_{\mathcal D}/a_{\mathcal D}=6\,\mathrm{Var}_w(H)-g\rho_{\mathcal D}$, so a spread of expansion rates alone can make the average accelerate; an exact two-patch example, checked in Lean, has $\ddot a_{\mathcal D}/a_{\mathcal D}=5/3$. Flat $\Lambda$CDM fitted to synthetic distances from such an ensemble, which contains no cosmological constant, returns $\Omega_\Lambda\approx0.60$, and a correction shaped by the generator's heterogeneity fits equally well, although neither fit is statistically adequate. On public DES SN5YR and DES Y6 BAO data the pipeline recovers the published supernova-only parameters, and a correction $A(1+z)^{-m}$ to $H^2$ settles at the boundary $m=0$, where it is a cosmological constant. Probe-split runs on DES Y3 and KiDS-450 two-point vectors validate the data layer only. A fundamental cosmological constant is not ruled out; the paper states exactly when a coarse description can close and which audits could separate a closure correction from a fundamental component.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{keywords:} dark energy; coarse-graining; cosmological backreaction; closure; posterior predictive checks; data compression; six birds; cosmology

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Dark energy enters cosmology as an extra component of the energy budget of a homogeneous Friedmann--Lema\^{i}tre--Robertson--Walker (FLRW) universe. The evidence for it comes from comparing model distances and clustering statistics with survey data, and the simplest description that fits is $\Lambda$CDM \cite{Riess1998SN,Perlmutter1999SN,Weinberg2013Acceleration}. One step in this procedure usually stays implicit. The fitted homogeneous model is treated as a \emph{closed} dynamical system: it is evolved on its own, at the coarse level, and its predictions are compared with data. The real universe is lumpy, and survey data products are compressed summaries of it. This paper asks when a coarse description of that kind can be a self-contained law, and what a fitted correction term means when it cannot.

Six Birds Theory (SBT) provides a vocabulary for this situation \cite{Tsiokos2026SixBirds,Tsiokos2026WakeStone}. A \emph{lens} specifies what an observer retains; a \emph{completion} specifies how the discarded detail is filled back in; together they define a \emph{packaging} operator that maps a detailed state to its coarse representative. A coarse theory is then a statement about how packaged states evolve. The natural diagnostic is \emph{route mismatch}: does ``package, then evolve'' agree with ``evolve, then package''? When the answer is no, one is tempted to conclude that the coarse law needs a correction term, and to read dark energy as such a correction.

The first contribution of this paper is to make that inference exact. We show that route mismatch on full states is the wrong criterion: it can be nonzero even when an exact coarse law exists, because it also detects whether the chosen representatives are invariant under evolution, which is a property of the completion rather than of the coarse dynamics. The exact criterion is \emph{fiber compatibility}: any two detailed states with the same observable must evolve into states with the same observable. Equivalently, route mismatch measured in observable space must vanish. When the criterion fails, no correction term on the same observables can be exact, and a fitted term such as $\Lambda$ is necessarily an effective description of the history actually realized. These statements are proved in Section~\ref{sec:framework} and machine-checked in Lean~4 \cite{Moura2021Lean4}.

The second contribution is a pair of mechanism models in which every quantity can be computed. In a nonlinear model observed through its mean, the observable mismatch is exactly the time step times the nonlinearity times the population variance. In an ensemble of Newtonian dust patches, each of which decelerates, the volume-averaged scale factor obeys an identity of the same algebraic form as the shear-free part of Buchert's averaged Raychaudhuri equation \cite{Buchert2000Dust,Buchert2001PerfectFluid}: the average accelerates whenever the volume-weighted variance of the local expansion rates exceeds a density threshold. We give an exact two-patch example, also checked in Lean, and show numerically what a homogeneous fit infers from such a generator.

The third contribution is a reproducible audit pipeline applied to public data. On DES SN5YR distances and the DES Y6 BAO likelihood we recover the published supernova-only parameters, test a simple family of corrections to the expansion rate, and run a cross-probe consistency check. For large-scale-structure (LSS) vectors from DES Y3 and KiDS-450 we build the data layer and probe-split bookkeeping, but we show that the scoring exercise run on them carries no cosmological information; it is reported only as a software validation.

\paragraph{What this paper does not claim.}
We do not claim that the cosmological constant is an artifact, and we do not propose a microphysical alternative. The toy models are not general-relativistic calculations, and the synthetic results establish a mechanism, not a property of the real universe. On current background data a constant correction and a fundamental $\Lambda$ are indistinguishable, and our LSS results contain no physics. \paragraph{Related work.}
Whether inhomogeneities can mimic or contribute to apparent acceleration has a long literature. Buchert's averaging formalism for dust and perfect fluids \cite{Buchert2000Dust,Buchert2001PerfectFluid} provides the kinematical framework, and Clarkson et al.\ \cite{Clarkson2011Averaging} review the averaging, backreaction and fitting problems. R\"as\"anen \cite{Rasanen2004Backreaction} and Kolb et al.\ \cite{Kolb2006Acceleration} explored scenarios in which backreaction is large, while Green and Wald \cite{GreenWald2014FLRW} argued that, under standard assumptions, FLRW remains an excellent approximation and backreaction cannot act as dark energy. Wiltshire's timescape model \cite{Wiltshire2007Cosmic} treats observers in voids and walls differently, and Buchert et al.\ \cite{Buchert2015Backreaction} summarize the state of the debate. We do not take a side on the magnitude of relativistic backreaction. Our contribution is a layer above it: an exact criterion for when a coarse description closes, and a set of audits that apply to any proposed coarse description and any data product.

\section{Closure framework}\label{sec:framework}

This section fixes notation and proves the facts used throughout. In cosmology the detailed state would be the matter and metric fields on a spatial slice and the observable would be a survey data product. We keep the definitions abstract and instantiate them concretely in the mechanism models of Section~\ref{sec:mech}.

\subsection{Lens, completion and packaging}\label{sec:framework:packaging}

Let $\StateSpace$ be a set of detailed (``micro'') states and $\ObsSpace$ a set of observables (``macro'' states).

\begin{definition}[Lens, completion, packaging]\label{def:packaging}
A \emph{lens} is a map $\Lens:\StateSpace\to\ObsSpace$ and a \emph{completion} is a map $\Completion:\ObsSpace\to\StateSpace$. The \emph{packaging operator} is
\begin{equation}
\PackageOp \;=\; \Completion\circ\Lens:\StateSpace\to\StateSpace .
\label{eq:packaging_def}
\end{equation}
The completion is \emph{lawful} if $\Lens(\Completion(y))=y$ for every $y\in\ObsSpace$, that is, if it reproduces the observable it was given.
\end{definition}

$\PackageOp(x)$ is the coarse representative of $x$: observe $x$ through the lens and fill in the rest according to the completion. In cosmology, $\Lens$ is set by the data reduction (a distance--redshift relation, a BAO dilation, a two-point vector), and $\Completion$ by the modeling choice (an FLRW background with a few parameters). Lawfulness is a hypothesis, not a property of arbitrary maps. The completions constructed explicitly in Section~\ref{sec:mech} are lawful. An FLRW fit, by contrast, generally reproduces its data only approximately; treating it as a lawful completion requires restricting attention to observables that the model family reproduces exactly.

\begin{proposition}[Packaging is idempotent]\label{prop:idempotent}
If the completion is lawful, then $\PackageOp\circ\PackageOp=\PackageOp$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\PackageOp(\PackageOp(x))=\Completion\big(\Lens(\Completion(\Lens(x)))\big)=\Completion(\Lens(x))=\PackageOp(x)$.
\end{proof}

When packaging is only approximately idempotent, as in floating-point arithmetic, we monitor the \emph{idempotence defect} $\iddef(x)=d_{\StateSpace}(\PackageOp(\PackageOp(x)),\PackageOp(x))$ for a chosen distance $d_{\StateSpace}$.

\subsection{When does a coarse law exist?}\label{sec:framework:descent}

Let $T:\StateSpace\to\StateSpace$ be the micro evolution over one time step.

\begin{definition}[Exact coarse law]\label{def:descent}
The evolution \emph{descends} to the observables if there is a map $S:\ObsSpace\to\ObsSpace$ with
\begin{equation}
\Lens\big(T(x)\big) \;=\; S\big(\Lens(x)\big)\qquad\text{for every } x\in\StateSpace .
\label{eq:descent}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The question is whether the next observable is determined by the current observable alone. The following theorem answers it.

\begin{theorem}[Descent criterion]\label{thm:descent}
Suppose the completion is lawful. The following are equivalent:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item[(i)] the evolution descends to the observables;
  \item[(ii)] \emph{fiber compatibility}: whenever $\Lens(x)=\Lens(x')$, also $\Lens(T(x))=\Lens(T(x'))$;
  \item[(iii)] $\PackageOp\circ T=\PackageOp\circ T\circ\PackageOp$.
\end{enumerate}
When they hold, the coarse law is $S=\Lens\circ T\circ\Completion$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
(i)$\Rightarrow$(ii): if $\Lens(x)=\Lens(x')$ then $\Lens(T(x))=S(\Lens(x))=S(\Lens(x'))=\Lens(T(x'))$. (ii)$\Rightarrow$(i): define $S=\Lens\circ T\circ\Completion$. Lawfulness gives $\Lens(\PackageOp(x))=\Lens(x)$, so $x$ and $\PackageOp(x)$ lie in the same fiber and (ii) yields $\Lens(T(x))=\Lens(T(\PackageOp(x)))=S(\Lens(x))$. (ii)$\Rightarrow$(iii): apply $\Completion$ to $\Lens(T(x))=\Lens(T(\PackageOp(x)))$. (iii)$\Rightarrow$(ii): apply $\Lens$ to (iii) and use lawfulness to get $\Lens(T(x))=\Lens(T(\PackageOp(x)))$ for every $x$; if $\Lens(x)=\Lens(x')$ then $\PackageOp(x)=\PackageOp(x')$, so $\Lens(T(x))=\Lens(T(\PackageOp(x)))=\Lens(T(\PackageOp(x')))=\Lens(T(x'))$.
\end{proof}

The implication (i)$\Rightarrow$(ii) does not use lawfulness. Hence a single pair $x,x'$ with $\Lens(x)=\Lens(x')$ but $\Lens(T(x))\neq\Lens(T(x'))$ rules out every coarse law $S$, whichever completion is used.

\paragraph{Two route-mismatch diagnostics.}
With a distance $d_{\StateSpace}$ on micro states and $d_{\ObsSpace}$ on observables, define
\begin{align}
\RM(x) &:= d_{\StateSpace}\big(\PackageOp(T(x)),\,T(\PackageOp(x))\big), \label{eq:rm_state}\\
\RMobs(x) &:= d_{\ObsSpace}\big(\Lens(T(x)),\,\Lens(T(\PackageOp(x)))\big). \label{eq:rm_obs}
\end{align}
For a lawful completion $\Lens(\PackageOp(T(x)))=\Lens(T(x))$, so $\RMobs$ is the lens image of the comparison made by $\RM$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic}a). The two diagnostics behave very differently.

\begin{proposition}[Commutation is sufficient, not necessary]\label{prop:commute}
Suppose the completion is lawful. If $\PackageOp\circ T=T\circ\PackageOp$, then the evolution descends. The converse is false.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
By Proposition~\ref{prop:idempotent}, $\PackageOp T\PackageOp=T\PackageOp\PackageOp=T\PackageOp=\PackageOp T$, which is condition (iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:descent}. For the converse, take $\StateSpace=\{0,1\}^2$, $\ObsSpace=\{0,1\}$, $\Lens(b,c)=b$, $\Completion(b)=(b,0)$ and $T(b,c)=(b,1)$. The completion is lawful and $\Lens\circ T=\Lens$, so $S=\mathrm{id}$ is an exact coarse law. Yet $\PackageOp(T(b,c))=(b,0)\neq(b,1)=T(\PackageOp(b,c))$ for every state.
\end{proof}

In the counterexample the evolution changes only a coordinate the lens cannot see, but it moves the completion's chosen representative $(b,0)$ to a different representative $(b,1)$ of the same fiber. State-space mismatch registers this change of representative even though nothing observable has happened. The observable diagnostic does not:

\begin{corollary}[Observable mismatch is an exact obstruction]\label{cor:obs}
Suppose the completion is lawful and $d_{\ObsSpace}(y,y)=0$ for all $y$. If $\RMobs(x)\neq0$ for some $x$, then the evolution does not descend. If $d_{\ObsSpace}$ is a metric, the evolution descends if and only if $\RMobs\equiv0$.
\end{corollary}
\begin{proof}
$x$ and $\PackageOp(x)$ lie in the same fiber, and $\RMobs(x)\neq0$ forces $\Lens(T(x))\neq\Lens(T(\PackageOp(x)))$, which violates fiber compatibility. If $d_{\ObsSpace}$ is a metric, $\RMobs\equiv0$ means $\Lens(T(x))=\Lens(T(\PackageOp(x)))$ for all $x$, which is condition (iii) of Theorem~\ref{thm:descent} after applying $\Completion$.
\end{proof}

A zero value of a pseudometric does not by itself imply equality, so with a pseudometric only the first statement is available. A minimal discrete example of an actual obstruction is the two-point sum lens $\Lens(x_1,x_2)=x_1+x_2$ on $\mathbb{N}^2$ with squaring evolution $T(x_1,x_2)=(x_1^2,x_2^2)$: the states $(0,2)$ and $(1,1)$ share the observable $2$ but evolve to observables $4$ and $2$, so no coarse law exists for any completion. Toy~1 in Section~\ref{sec:mech:toy1} is the continuous version of this example.

\subsection{What a correction term can and cannot do}\label{sec:framework:rewrite}

SBT calls a modification of the coarse evolution an \emph{operator rewrite} (its primitive P1). Theorem~\ref{thm:descent} sharpens what such a rewrite can achieve. If fiber compatibility holds, the exact coarse law $S=\Lens\circ T\circ\Completion$ already exists, and a ``rewrite'' amounts to identifying $S$ correctly; for instance, a homogeneous law may simply have been written down wrongly. If fiber compatibility fails, then no map $S$ on the same observables reproduces the observable evolution of every micro state. Three responses remain:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{refine the lens}: retain more coarse variables (a variance, a second fluid, a history) until the enlarged observable is fiber compatible;
  \item \emph{accept an effective law}: choose $\widetilde S$ that is accurate on the states that actually occur, for example along one realized history, without being exact for all states;
  \item \emph{change the micro model}.
\end{enumerate}
If the observables used in cosmology fail fiber compatibility, fitting a homogeneous model to data is an instance of the second response: the universe realizes one history, and a fitted term such as $\Lambda$ or a correction $\Delta_H(z)$ can match that history without being an exact closure. Fitting alone establishes neither fiber compatibility nor its failure, and a good fit to one trajectory is not evidence of a closed coarse law. Distinguishing an effective correction from a fundamental component requires predictions that reach beyond the fitted history, which is the role of the audits discussed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

\section{Mechanism models}\label{sec:mech}

The two models in this section are deliberately simple. Each makes one link in the argument fully computable: the first shows how nonlinearity plus averaging breaks fiber compatibility, and the second shows how a population of decelerating regions can have an accelerating average.

\subsection{Toy 1: nonlinear evolution seen through a mean}\label{sec:mech:toy1}

Let $x\in\mathbb{R}^n$ evolve componentwise by
\begin{equation}
T_i(x) \;=\; x_i+\Delta t\,\big(\alpha x_i-\beta x_i^2\big),
\label{eq:toy1_dynamics}
\end{equation}
observed through its mean $\Lens(x)=\bar x=\tfrac1n\sum_i x_i$, with the completion $\Completion(\bar x)=(\bar x,\dots,\bar x)$. This completion is lawful, so packaging is exactly idempotent. Write $v(x)=\tfrac1n\sum_i(x_i-\bar x)^2$ for the population variance. Averaging Eq.~\eqref{eq:toy1_dynamics} gives
\begin{equation}
\Lens\big(T(x)\big)=\bar x+\Delta t\big(\alpha\bar x-\beta(\bar x^2+v)\big),\qquad
\Lens\big(T(\PackageOp(x))\big)=\bar x+\Delta t\big(\alpha\bar x-\beta\bar x^2\big),
\end{equation}
so with $d_{\ObsSpace}(y,y')=|y-y'|$,
\begin{equation}
\RMobs(x) \;=\; |\Delta t\,\beta|\;v(x).
\label{eq:toy1_identity}
\end{equation}
Three conclusions follow (with $\Delta t>0$ and $n\ge2$). In the linear case $\beta=0$ the mean obeys the exact coarse law $\bar x\mapsto(1+\alpha\Delta t)\bar x$. If $\beta\neq0$, every state with nonzero spread is a fiber witness, so by Corollary~\ref{cor:obs} no law for the mean alone exists. And the obstruction is quantitative: it is the nonlinearity times the information the lens discards. Refining the lens does not easily cure this. For two components the mean and variance determine the state up to ordering and do evolve in closed form, but for the population simulated below, adding the variance to the lens does not close the system, because the evolved variance depends on the third and fourth moments; this is the usual closure problem of moment hierarchies.

We simulate $n=1000$ components drawn uniformly from $[0,1]$, with $\alpha=0$, $\Delta t=0.5$ and 200 steps, for $\beta\in\{0,0.25,0.5,1,2\}$. Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}a shows the maximum observable mismatch over each run. It is zero for $\beta=0$ and grows linearly with $\beta$, taking the value $\Delta t\,\beta\,v_0$ set by the initial variance $v_0\approx0.081$ (for $\beta=2$, $8.10\times10^{-2}$). The idempotence defect, measured as a Euclidean distance between micro states, stays below $10^{-14}$ in every run, consistent with exact idempotence up to rounding.

\subsection{Toy 2: decelerating patches, accelerating average}\label{sec:mech:toy2}

\paragraph{Model.}
Consider $N$ disjoint patches, each a Newtonian dust region with scale factor $a_i(t)$, density $\rho_i=\rho_{i0}a_i^{-3}$ and
\begin{equation}
\ddot a_i \;=\; -\frac{g}{3}\,\rho_i\,a_i,
\label{eq:toy2_accel}
\end{equation}
where $g$ plays the role of $4\pi G$. Every patch decelerates. Equation~\eqref{eq:toy2_accel} has the first integral
\begin{equation}
H_i^2 \;=\; \frac{2g}{3}\,\rho_i+\frac{\kappa_i}{a_i^2},\qquad H_i=\dot a_i/a_i,
\label{eq:toy2_friedmann}
\end{equation}
with a constant $\kappa_i$ that plays the role of negative spatial curvature. This is the Friedmann equation with $8\pi G/3=2g/3$. Patches start at $a_i(0)=1$ in two populations: a fraction $f_{\rm v}$ of ``voids'' with density $\bar\rho(1-\delta_\rho)$, and ``walls'' with the density that keeps the mean equal to $\bar\rho$. Voids receive $\kappa_i=\kappa_{\rm s}(\bar\rho-\rho_{i0})/\bar\rho=\kappa_{\rm s}\delta_\rho\ge0$ (positive when $\kappa_{\rm s},\delta_\rho>0$); walls have $\kappa_i=0$.

\paragraph{Volume average.}
The lens is the volume average. Let $V=\sum_i a_i^3$, $a_{\mathcal D}=(V/N)^{1/3}$ and $w_i=a_i^3/V$. The domain expansion rate is $H_{\mathcal D}=\dot a_{\mathcal D}/a_{\mathcal D}=\sum_i w_iH_i$ and the domain density is $\rho_{\mathcal D}=\sum_i w_i\rho_i=\sum_i\rho_{i0}/V$. Differentiating $V$ twice and using Eqs.~\eqref{eq:toy2_accel}--\eqref{eq:toy2_friedmann} gives the exact identity
\begin{equation}
3\,\frac{\ddot a_{\mathcal D}}{a_{\mathcal D}} \;=\; 6\,\mathrm{Var}_w(H_i)\;-\;g\,\rho_{\mathcal D},
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}_w(H_i)=\sum_i w_i\,(H_i-H_{\mathcal D})^2 .
\label{eq:toy2_identity}
\end{equation}
A single homogeneous dust region satisfies $3\ddot a/a=-g\rho$. We therefore define the closure term
\begin{equation}
Q \;:=\; 3\,\frac{\ddot a_{\mathcal D}}{a_{\mathcal D}}+g\,\rho_{\mathcal D} \;=\; 6\,\mathrm{Var}_w(H_i)\;\ge 0,
\label{eq:toy2_Q}
\end{equation}
which measures how far the average departs from homogeneous dust. Two points are worth stating explicitly. First, $Q>0$ alone does not mean acceleration; the average accelerates when $Q>g\rho_{\mathcal D}$, that is, when the spread of expansion rates outweighs the mean density. Second, Eq.~\eqref{eq:toy2_identity} has the same form as Buchert's averaged Raychaudhuri equation for irrotational dust with $\Lambda=0$, $3\ddot a_{\mathcal D}/a_{\mathcal D}=-4\pi G\langle\rho\rangle_{\mathcal D}+\mathcal Q_{\mathcal D}$ with $\mathcal Q_{\mathcal D}=\tfrac23(\langle\theta^2\rangle_{\mathcal D}-\langle\theta\rangle_{\mathcal D}^2)-2\langle\sigma^2\rangle_{\mathcal D}$ \cite{Buchert2000Dust}: for patches with expansion scalar $\theta_i=3H_i$ and no shear, the two coincide. The toy model is nevertheless not a relativistic calculation. Its patches do not interact, it has no shear, no metric and no light propagation, and nothing here bears on the size of backreaction in the real universe \cite{GreenWald2014FLRW}.

\paragraph{An exact example.}
Take two patches with $g=\bar\rho=1$, $f_{\rm v}=\delta_\rho=1/2$ and $\kappa_{\rm s}=52/3$. Then the densities are $(1/2,3/2)$, the curvature constants $(26/3,0)$, and Eq.~\eqref{eq:toy2_friedmann} gives initial expansion rates $(3,1)$. Both patches decelerate, with $\ddot a_i=(-1/6,-1/2)$, but $\mathrm{Var}_w(H)=1$ and $g\rho_{\mathcal D}=1$, so $3\ddot a_{\mathcal D}/a_{\mathcal D}=6-1=5$ and $\ddot a_{\mathcal D}/a_{\mathcal D}=5/3>0$. These rational equations are checked in Lean, and the simulation code reproduces them to rounding. By continuity the average keeps accelerating for some interval after $t=0$; we claim nothing about later times.

\paragraph{Simulation.}
We integrate $N=500$ patches with fourth-order Runge--Kutta (time step $0.02$, 1200 steps, $f_{\rm v}=0.3$, $\delta_\rho=0.9$, $\kappa_{\rm s}=5$, $g=\bar\rho=1$) and compare with a homogeneous control ($\delta_\rho=\kappa_{\rm s}=0$). The residual of the first integral, Eq.~\eqref{eq:toy2_friedmann}, normalized by each patch's initial squared velocity, stays below $3.2\times10^{-9}$, and $Q$ computed from Eq.~\eqref{eq:toy2_Q} agrees with a direct evaluation of $\ddot V$ to $10^{-14}$. In the control, $Q$ vanishes to $10^{-30}$. In the heterogeneous run, $Q$ starts at $1.74$, and $Q/(g\rho_{\mathcal D})$ stays above one for most of the run (Fig.~\ref{fig:toy2}a): the domain average accelerates at 99.8\% of all recorded times and at 98.8\% of the times in the last fifth of the run, even though every patch decelerates throughout (Fig.~\ref{fig:toy2}b). Changing the random seed only permutes which patches are voids; because the averages are permutation invariant, every seed yields the same domain history.

\subsection{Staging: dependence on the lens scale}\label{sec:mech:staging}\label{sec:results:staging}

Closure diagnostics depend on how coarse the lens is, which SBT calls staging (P4). To illustrate this, we take $N=500$ patches with densities spread uniformly over $\bar\rho(1\pm0.8)$, sort them by initial density, and let the lens average the scale factors and velocities over consecutive blocks of $L$ patches. The completion assigns each patch its block averages. We measure the state-space mismatch $\RM$ (root-mean-square over the $2N$ components of the state) for one Runge--Kutta step and average it over the last fifth of 250 steps. Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}b shows that it rises steadily from exactly zero at $L=1$ (no coarse-graining) to $1.3\times10^{-4}$ at $L=500$ (a single domain), while the idempotence defect stays at rounding level for every $L$. This is a state-space diagnostic, so by Section~\ref{sec:framework:descent} it mixes genuine obstruction with changes of representative; we use it only to show that a closure diagnostic is a function of the chosen lens scale, not a single number attached to the system.

\section{Synthetic inference: what a homogeneous fit sees}\label{sec:synthetic}

We now treat the domain history of Toy~2 as a mock universe and ask what a homogeneous model infers from it. The generator contains matter and curvature-like terms but no cosmological constant.

\paragraph{Mock data.}
We normalize $a_{\mathcal D}=1$ at the final time, define $1+z=1/a_{\mathcal D}$, and use the domain expansion rate $H_{\mathcal D}(z)$ to compute the comoving distance
\begin{equation}
\chi(z) \;=\; \int_0^z\frac{\dd z'}{H(z')}\qquad(c=1).
\label{eq:chi_def}
\end{equation}
Forty mock points on an even grid in $0.05\le z\le2$ receive Gaussian noise with $\sigma_k=10^{-4}+0.005\,\chi(z_k)$. Different noise seeds share the same generator history (Section~\ref{sec:mech:toy2}) and differ only in the noise realization.

\paragraph{Homogeneous fits.}
We fit, by weighted least squares, (a) flat matter-only FLRW with free $H_0$ and (b) flat $\Lambda$CDM with free $H_0$ and $\Omega_\Lambda\in[0,0.95]$. For the noise realization shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:synthetic} and Table~\ref{tab:synth_inference}, $\Lambda$CDM returns $\Omega_\Lambda=0.602$ and lowers $\chi^2$ from $8.59\times10^{3}$ to $380$. A second realization gives the same $\Omega_\Lambda=0.602$ and lowers $\chi^2$ from $8.47\times10^{3}$ to $253$. In both cases the difference in the Akaike information criterion (AIC, $\chi^2+2k$ for $k$ fitted parameters \cite{Akaike1974AIC}) is about $8.2\times10^3$. A homogeneous model, forced to describe the averaged history of a population of decelerating patches, thus infers a substantial cosmological constant. The fit is far better than matter-only but is not statistically adequate: with 40 points and two parameters one expects $\chi^2\approx38$, and the residuals in Fig.~\ref{fig:synthetic} are visibly structured. This is a best-fit point under a misspecified model, not a detection.

\paragraph{A heterogeneity-shaped correction.}
In the spirit of Section~\ref{sec:framework:rewrite} we replace $\Lambda$ with a correction tied to the generator. Let $g(z)$ be the volume-weighted variance $\mathrm{Var}_w(H_i)$ of the generator mapped to redshift and normalized to unit maximum, and fit
\begin{equation}
H(z)^2 \;=\; H_0^2\,(1+z)^3 \;-\; A\,g(z),\qquad A\ge0,
\label{eq:rewrite_synthetic}
\end{equation}
with free $H_0$ and $A$. Here $H_0^2$ is the coefficient of the matter term, not the present expansion rate. The correction is subtractive and lowers $H^2$ below its matter term, by more at higher redshift because $g(z)$ grows toward the past. (Flat $\Lambda$CDM likewise lies below flat matter-only at $z>0$ when the present expansion rate is held fixed; at a fixed matter coefficient a positive $\Lambda$ would instead raise $H^2$.) The fit enforces $H^2>0$ on the whole redshift interval, by an exact maximization over the piecewise-linear shape rather than a check at sample points. The correction matches $\Lambda$CDM with the same number of parameters ($\chi^2=378.6$ versus $379.6$; Table~\ref{tab:synth_inference}, Fig.~\ref{fig:synthetic}) and is equally inadequate statistically. The shape $g(z)$ is supplied from the generator, not inferred from the distances.

\paragraph{Amplitude versus heterogeneity.}
Repeating the correction fit for generators with $\delta_\rho=0.3$, $0.6$ and $0.9$ (300 patches each) gives a fitted amplitude at its lower bound, $A\approx0$ to rounding, for the two weaker settings and $A=844$ for the strongest. The two lower amplitudes are numerically indistinguishable from zero. Their unrounded optimizer outputs ($1.6\times10^{-18}$ and $2.4\times10^{-16}$) happen to be ordered, which makes the rank correlation formally one, but with three points its exact two-sided permutation $p$-value is $1/3$ (treating the two near-zero values as tied would give $2/3$). The sweep shows only that the correction switches on for strong heterogeneity in this setting; it does not establish a scaling law.

\paragraph{A calibration check, not a prediction.}
One can also ask whether a fitted amplitude recovers the heterogeneity profile itself. We calibrate the template $\mathrm{Var}_w(H_i)(z)$ and a reference amplitude on one noise realization and predict the profile on three others as $(A_{\rm fit}/A_{\rm ref})$ times the template, or zero if $A_{\rm fit}<0.1$. The median ratio of root-mean-square errors, correction versus $\Lambda$CDM, is $8.8\times10^{-3}$. This number should not be read as predictive success. All four realizations share one generator history, the template is supplied from that history, and $\Lambda$CDM predicts zero variance by construction. The check confirms only that the calibration chain is internally consistent.

\section{Public background probes}\label{sec:background}

We next run the same pipeline on two compact public likelihoods: the DES 5-year supernova (SN5YR) Hubble diagram \cite{DES2024SN5YRCosmology,DES2024SN5YRData} and the DES Y6 angular BAO measurement \cite{DES2024Y6BAO}. These probes constrain the expansion history without requiring an LSS theory code.

\subsection{Likelihoods}\label{sec:background:likes}

\paragraph{Supernovae.}
The release provides distance moduli $\mu^{\rm obs}$ for 1829 supernovae, per-object uncertainties, and a systematics covariance. Following the release documentation, the statistical-only covariance file is identically zero and the statistical errors are the per-object uncertainties, so the total covariance is $C_{\rm SN}=C_{\rm stat+sys}+\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{\mu}^2)$; it is positive definite without regularization. Distances use the Hubble-diagram redshift $z_{\rm HD}$ for the radial integral and the heliocentric redshift $z_{\rm hel}$ in the luminosity prefactor, $D_L=(1+z_{\rm hel})\,D_M(z_{\rm HD})$, as in the release's own likelihood. The model is $\mu^{\rm th}=5\log_{10}(D_L/\mathrm{Mpc})+25+\mathcal M$ with $H_0$ fixed at $70\ \mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}$; the offset $\mathcal M$ absorbs the absolute magnitude and the $H_0$ scale and is profiled analytically,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathcal M}(\theta)=\frac{\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}C_{\rm SN}^{-1}\Delta(\theta)}{\mathbf 1^{\mathsf T}C_{\rm SN}^{-1}\mathbf 1},\qquad \Delta(\theta)=\mu^{\rm obs}-\mu^{\rm th}(\theta,\mathcal M{=}0).
\label{eq:sn_Mhat}
\end{equation}
Supernovae therefore constrain the shape of the expansion history, not its scale. Information criteria count $\mathcal M$ as a fitted parameter.

\paragraph{BAO.}
The DES Y6 release provides $\Delta\chi^2(\alpha)$ curves for the dilation parameter $\alpha$ at $z_{\rm eff}=0.851$, separately for three correlated clustering statistics and as a consensus curve. Because the three statistics are measured on the same galaxies, adding their $\chi^2$ values would double-count information; we use only the consensus curve, whose minimum is $\hat\alpha=0.9571$ with $\Delta\chi^2=1$ half-width $0.0201$. We model
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{\rm th}(\theta)=\frac{D_M(z_{\rm eff};\theta)}{D_M^{\rm fid}(z_{\rm eff})},
\label{eq:alpha_th}
\end{equation}
where the reference $D_M^{\rm fid}$ is computed in the template cosmology of the release (flat, $\Omega_m=0.31$, $h=0.676$) \cite{DES2024Y6BAO}, and the sound horizon and $H_0$ of the model are both held at their template values. Holding the sound horizon fixed alone would not remove the $H_0$ dependence, since $D_M\propto1/H_0$. Outside the tabulated range of $\alpha$ the likelihood is zero rather than extrapolated. This one-number BAO likelihood is a geometry anchor, not a full BAO analysis with a self-consistent sound horizon. Combined fits add the SN and BAO $\chi^2$ values, which assumes the two probes are independent.

\subsection{Fits}\label{sec:background:fits}

Table~\ref{tab:background_fits} summarizes the fits. For SN alone, flat $\Lambda$CDM gives $\Omega_m=0.352$ with $\chi^2=1640.1$ for 1829 objects, and flat $w$CDM gives $w=-0.80$ with $\Omega_m=0.267$. Both agree with the supernova-only values published by the collaboration \cite{DES2024SN5YRCosmology}. The improvement in $\chi^2$ from freeing $w$ (1.6) does not pay for the extra parameter in AIC. With BAO alone, a single number cannot separate models: flat $\Lambda$CDM and an open model without $\Lambda$ both fit the consensus curve almost perfectly ($\Delta\mathrm{AIC}=0.07$).

\paragraph{A decaying correction.}
To test a correction of the type discussed in Section~\ref{sec:framework:rewrite} on real data, we fit
\begin{equation}
H(z)^2=H_0^2\big[\Omega_m(1+z)^3+\Omega_k(1+z)^2+A\,(1+z)^{-m}\big],\qquad \Omega_k=1-\Omega_m-A,
\label{eq:rewrite_public}
\end{equation}
with $\Omega_m\in[0.05,1.5]$, $A\in[0,2]$ and $m\in[0,6]$. Unlike Eq.~\eqref{eq:rewrite_synthetic} this correction is additive. For $m=0$ it is a cosmological constant with free curvature. For $m>0$ it decays toward the past. In the flat case $A=1-\Omega_m$ it is a fluid with constant equation of state $w=-1-m/3$, so the declared range $m\ge0$ covers only $w\le-1$. Positivity of $H^2$ is enforced on the whole integration interval through an exact characterization of the possible minima of Eq.~\eqref{eq:rewrite_public}, not by checking sample points. To avoid optimizer artifacts near the hard edge of the BAO likelihood, each fit takes the best of several bounded searches started at $m\in\{0,1,3,6\}$, together with the exact embedding of the best $\Lambda$CDM fit.

For both SN and SN+BAO the best fit sits at the boundary $m=0$, where the correction reproduces $\Lambda$CDM with free curvature exactly. It attains the same $\chi^2$ and pays the AIC penalty of 2 for the extra parameter. The correct reading is that the data do not prefer a correction that decays toward the past. They do not single out a constant: the family cannot represent a correction that grows toward the past, which is the direction ($w>-1$) favored by the SN-only $w$CDM fit. With background geometry alone, a constant closure correction and a fundamental $\Lambda$ are indistinguishable, and the curvature direction is poorly constrained ($\Omega_k\approx0.15$ in these fits, with $H_0$ fixed).

\subsection{Cross-probe check}\label{sec:background:ppd}

A model fitted to one probe should predict another. Training flat $\Lambda$CDM on SN ($\Omega_m=0.352$) predicts $\alpha=0.9790$, which is $0.0219$, or $1.1$ half-widths, above the BAO consensus value; the BAO curve assigns this prediction $\Delta\chi^2=1.17$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:background}). In the reverse direction, BAO alone prefers $\Omega_m=0.400$, which raises the SN $\chi^2$ by 7.5 relative to the SN best fit. This reverse score re-profiles $\mathcal M$ on the supernovae themselves, so it tests only the shape of the predicted Hubble diagram. Both directions are point comparisons that ignore parameter uncertainty, and they show the mild tension between the two probes expected for a fixed-$H_0$, fixed-sound-horizon treatment. They are not calibrated posterior predictive $p$-values \cite{Gelman1996PPD}.

\section{Large-scale-structure data layer}\label{sec:lss}

Background geometry cannot separate a constant correction from $\Lambda$, so growth and clustering probes are the natural next step. We have built the data layer for that step but not the physics. The pipeline ingests the public DES Y3 two-point (maglim) vector \cite{DES2022Y3_3x2pt} and a KiDS-450 bandpower vector \cite{vanUitert2018KiDSGAMA,KiDSDataProducts}. For DES Y3 it orders every element according to the spectra and offsets declared in the FITS headers; for KiDS-450 it follows the block ordering documented in the release (50 shear, 40 galaxy--galaxy-lensing and 10 clustering bandpowers). Each element is assigned to a probe, statistic, tomographic bin pair and scale (angular separation for DES, multipole for KiDS). For DES Y3 this gives 400 cosmic-shear, 480 galaxy--galaxy-lensing (GGL) and 120 clustering elements (Appendix~\ref{app:lss}, Fig.~\ref{fig:y3_blockmap}). Masks for probe splits and scale cuts are then index selections on this map, and all $\chi^2$ evaluations use Cholesky solves of the corresponding sub-covariances without regularization.

No physical $3\times2$pt theory code is used. The probe-split exercises we ran replace the theory prediction with the measured vector plus a prescribed offset built from the covariance and the block metadata. In such a construction the measured central values cancel from every residual. We verified that the scores do not change when the data vector is altered. The exercises therefore test masking, fitting, scoring and provenance on realistic dimensions and covariances, and nothing else. We report them in Appendix~\ref{app:lss} as software validation. A physical analysis would also need a calibrated predictive check: for jointly Gaussian blocks $A$ and $B$, held-out residuals should be scored against the conditional covariance $C_{BB}-C_{BA}C_{AA}^{-1}C_{AB}$ after subtracting $C_{BA}C_{AA}^{-1}r_A$, with parameter uncertainty propagated. A shear-only path through an external Limber-integral code is implemented but was unavailable in the baseline environment, and no result from it is reported.

\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What dark energy means in a closure-first view}\label{sec:discussion:rewrite}

The standard reading of $\Lambda$CDM treats dark energy as a component of the universe. The closure-first reading treats a fitted dark-energy term as the correction needed for a homogeneous coarse description to track the averaged history of an inhomogeneous one. Section~\ref{sec:framework} makes this reading precise. If the chosen coarse variables were fiber compatible, an exact law on those variables would exist, fixed by the micro dynamics (Theorem~\ref{thm:descent}); whether that law takes the FLRW form, or corresponds to a homogeneous solution of Einstein's equations, would need separate justification. If they are not, as Toy~1 illustrates for a simple nonlinear model, then no correction on the same variables is exact, and a fitted term describes the realized history rather than a closed law. The patch ensemble shows that such a term can have the sign of acceleration even when every region decelerates, and the synthetic fits show that a homogeneous model reports it as $\Omega_\Lambda>0$.

None of this decides the real case. The relevant question is quantitative: how large is the closure correction for the actual universe, its actual lens, and general relativity? Green and Wald argue that it is small \cite{GreenWald2014FLRW}, while others argue that it may not be \cite{Rasanen2004Backreaction,Kolb2006Acceleration,Wiltshire2007Cosmic,Buchert2015Backreaction}. Our framework does not settle that debate. It does say what any resolution must establish: either fiber compatibility of the chosen observables to the required accuracy, or an effective correction whose consequences beyond the fitted history can be tested.

\subsection{What would distinguish a closure correction from \texorpdfstring{$\Lambda$}{Lambda}}\label{sec:discussion:tests}

A correction can always be added to fit one probe, so fit quality alone cannot discriminate. Discriminating power has to come from predictions outside the fitted history. Four kinds of evidence would count.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Calibrated cross-probe prediction} (P6). Fit on one probe and predict another with a physical theory code, using conditional Gaussian predictive checks with parameter uncertainty. A closure correction tied to a lens has no reason to transfer between lenses as a single constant does.
  \item \emph{Staging dependence} (P4). Repeat inference under different scale cuts, smoothing scales and summary statistics. A fundamental constant should show no structured dependence beyond known systematics. A closure correction may, although a constant correction would not, and systematics or model misspecification can mimic the effect, so staging dependence is a hypothesis to test rather than a signature in itself.
  \item \emph{Independent heterogeneity proxies} (P3). If inferred corrections track independent measures of inhomogeneity across sub-samples, a closure origin gains support.
  \item \emph{Parsimony} (P1). Added flexibility should be justified by out-of-sample improvement, not by in-sample $\chi^2$.
\end{enumerate}
The data layer and provenance infrastructure described here are built to support these tests, with every run recorded together with its configuration, metrics and provenance. Physical fitting with a theory code, conditional predictive checks and the propagation of parameter uncertainty still have to be implemented.

\subsection{Limitations}\label{sec:discussion:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item The mechanism models are Newtonian and shear-free with non-interacting patches. The resemblance of Eq.~\eqref{eq:toy2_identity} to Buchert's equation is structural, not a relativistic result.
  \item The synthetic fits are best-fit points under misspecified models; the heterogeneity shape is supplied from the generator; and the calibration check reuses one generator history.
  \item The background analysis fixes $H_0$ and the BAO sound horizon, uses a one-number BAO likelihood, and treats SN and BAO as independent. The correction family covers only corrections that are constant or decay toward the past.
  \item The LSS results are software validation only. Physical $3\times2$pt likelihoods, including the DES Y6 $3\times2$pt release, are not analyzed here.
  \item All optimizations report the best of declared bounded searches and are not certified global optima. Floating-point checks are not interval certificates.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

A homogeneous cosmological model is a coarse description of an inhomogeneous universe, and whether it can be a closed law is a mathematical question with a precise answer. An exact coarse law exists exactly when micro states that share an observable evolve to states that share an observable; route mismatch measured on the observables with a metric distance detects any failure, whereas route mismatch measured on full states can raise false alarms. When closure fails, a fitted correction such as $\Lambda$ is an effective description of the realized history. Simple models show that such a correction can mimic acceleration while every region decelerates, and that a homogeneous fit reports it as a cosmological constant. Current public background data are consistent with a constant correction and cannot tell it apart from a fundamental $\Lambda$. The tools needed to go further, a formally checked closure criterion, mechanism models, and a provenance-tracked audit pipeline with an LSS data layer ready for physical likelihoods, are in place; the decisive tests are cross-probe and staging audits with physical theory codes.

\section{LSS probe-split exercises (software validation)}\label{app:lss}

This appendix documents the LSS exercises referred to in Section~\ref{sec:lss}. None of them is evidence about cosmology.

\paragraph{Construction.}
For a data vector $y$ with covariance $C$ the exercises use a reference vector $t_0=y+q$, where $q=0.1\,\sigma_i\,(1+k_{p(i)}+0.1\,\hat x_i)$ is built from the diagonal uncertainties $\sigma_i$, a fixed per-probe constant $k_p$ (0 for shear, $0.4$ for clustering, $-0.2$ for GGL) and the standardized scale coordinate $\hat x_i$ (angular separation for DES, multipole $\ell$ for KiDS). Table~\ref{tab:lss_protocol} uses exactly these coefficients. A one-template family ($t_0+\phi_1 b_1$, with $b_1\propto\sigma$) and a two-template family (adding $b_2\propto\sigma\,\hat x$) are fitted by generalized least squares on a training block and scored on the held-out block with $\chi^2=r_B^{\mathsf T}C_{BB}^{-1}r_B$. The residual is $y-t_0-B\phi=-q-B\phi$, so the measured values $y$ cancel. The template bases are fixed before the split and rank-deficient training bases are rejected. Rescaling a basis column does not change any score, but rescaling $q$ does.

\paragraph{Results.}
Table~\ref{tab:lss_protocol} lists the held-out scores for the shear versus non-shear split on DES Y3 and KiDS-450. On DES Y3 we also varied the split (shear versus the rest, alternating and low/high tomographic bins), the two injection coefficients (each over $\{0.05,0.10\}$), subsampled probe blocks, and perturbed the covariance diagonal. Of 30 scenarios, 26 completed, 4 were skipped because too few points remained, and none failed; a scenario is counted only if every model fit in it succeeds. For the canonical direction, shear $\to$ clustering + GGL (nine completed scenarios), the median improvement of the two-template over the one-template family is $5.7\times10^{-3}$ in $\chi^2$, with interquartile range $1.9\times10^{-2}$. A scale-cut sweep that removes small angular scales in eight steps selects the same cut ($\theta_{\min}\approx25.4$ arcmin) for every family and direction. All of these numbers reflect the injected offset $q$, not the sky.

A variant that adds the SN and BAO likelihoods to the training objective was also run. Several of its three-parameter fits return their starting point, at the lower bound of one parameter, so its scores are not reported.

\section{Formal verification}\label{app:lean}

The Lean~4 development (toolchain v4.31.0) in \texttt{lean/} formalizes lenses, completions, packaging, exact zero and nonzero route mismatch, idempotence, lawful completions, fiber compatibility and exact descent. Table~\ref{tab:lean} maps the statements of this paper to Lean declarations. The project builds without \texttt{sorry}, local axioms or \texttt{native\_decide}. All declarations in Table~\ref{tab:lean} except the last depend on no axioms. The two-patch example uses Lean's standard axioms (\texttt{propext}, \texttt{Classical.choice}, \texttt{Quot.sound}) through the core rational-number library, and its arithmetic is checked by the kernel.

The formalization covers the abstract closure mathematics, the explicit counterexamples, and the exact initial-state arithmetic of the two-patch example. It does not cover the real-valued Toy~1 dynamics, ordinary differential equations, general relativity, numerical integration, or any part of the statistical pipeline. The Lean statements about distances use only $d(x,x)=0$; zero readout from a pseudometric is never taken to imply equality.

\section{Reproducibility}\label{app:repro}

The repository \cite{sixbirds_de_repo} is organized so that every number and figure in this paper can be traced to a recorded run.

\paragraph{Data.} External datasets are fetched through a registry (\path{data/registry.yaml}) with \path{scripts/fetch_data.py}. Each fetch records the source URL, expected and observed hashes, size, time and platform, and a repeated fetch is a cache hit when the hashes match.

\paragraph{Runs.} Every experiment writes a run folder \path{results/<experiment>/<timestamp>_<commit>/} containing the resolved configuration, a metrics file, a provenance record (commit, cleanliness of the working tree, package versions, seed) and its plots and tables. The runs used in this paper were made from clean working trees, at commit \texttt{39346c3} for the four runs that use the BAO likelihood and at commit \texttt{bfa9bde} for all others (the code they exercise is identical at both commits); hashes of the latter are collected in \path{research/mathematical_audit/completion_evidence.json}.

\paragraph{Paper artifacts.} Tables are generated from run metrics by \path{scripts/vendor_tables.py}, and figures by \path{scripts/make_paper_figures.py}. Where a plotted series is not stored in a run folder, the figure script recomputes it from the run's configuration (supplemented, for the synthetic distances, by the ensemble size and duration fixed in the runner) and stops if the recomputation does not reproduce the run's recorded metrics. Both scripts write provenance files next to their outputs. Table~\ref{tab:evidence_map} lists the source of each artifact.

\paragraph{Commands.} Run folders are not tracked in git. To regenerate everything from a clean clone (the public-data targets fetch their datasets through the registry):
\begin{verbatim}
pip install -e ".[dev]"
make exp-toy1 exp-toy2 exp-scale-sweep exp-infer-distance \
     exp-rewrite-vs-lambda exp-ppd-synthetic
make exp-public-evidence-background exp-public-evidence-lss \
     exp-kids450-ppd-probe-split
\end{verbatim}
This creates new, timestamped run folders. Point the run-folder entries at the top of \path{scripts/vendor_tables.py} and \path{scripts/make_paper_figures.py} to them, then build the artifacts and the paper:
\begin{verbatim}
PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/vendor_tables.py
PYTHONPATH=src python scripts/make_paper_figures.py
make paper
cd lean && lake build && lake env lean Audit.lean
\end{verbatim}
The paper build requires a \LaTeX\ installation with BibTeX (\texttt{latexmk} is used when available).
The test suite (\texttt{pytest}, 97 tests) includes exact-algebra controls for both toy identities, analytic counterexamples for the positivity checks, covariance and ordering regressions, and a check that the LSS scores are invariant to the measured data vector.

\section{Version history}\label{app:versions}

\paragraph{Version 1 (5 February 2026)} and \textbf{Version 2 (10 February 2026)}: original release, and an editorial revision adding declarations, related work and reproducibility material.

\paragraph{Version 3 (14 October 2026).} Following a mathematical audit of the code and proofs:
the closure criterion is stated as fiber compatibility, with commutation on full states shown to be sufficient but not necessary for closure, and mismatch on observables shown to be an exact obstruction (new Lean proofs and counterexample);
the patch model's Friedmann coefficient ($2g/3$) and volume-weighted density are corrected, and the identity $Q=6\,\mathrm{Var}_w(H_i)$ and an exact two-patch example (in Lean) are added;
the synthetic distances are identified as comoving, the synthetic correction as subtractive, and the synthetic fits are no longer described as adequate, with exact significance for the amplitude sweep;
the supernova likelihood uses heliocentric redshifts in the luminosity prefactor, the BAO likelihood uses only the released consensus curve, referenced to the release's template cosmology with $H_0$ fixed, and background fits use multi-start optimization with whole-interval positivity checks, which changes several reported numbers but not the constant-correction result;
the LSS probe-split exercises are reclassified as software validation because their scores do not depend on the measured data;
all figures and tables are regenerated from clean, recorded runs.
